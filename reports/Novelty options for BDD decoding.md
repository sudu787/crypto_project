# Measure the decoding step, stop reinventing decoders

The team's novelty does not lie in the decoder itself. Every ingredient of VA-ANP has a published ancestor: per-level plane counts from ||b*_i|| and sigma (Lindner-Peikert 2011), per-node target-dependent plane choice (Klein, Liu-Ling-Stehlé, threshold Schnorr-Euchner sphere decoding), breadth-first lists pruned relative to the best metric (the T-algorithm, threshold K-best), and sphere or radius pruning (Gama-Nguyen-Regev, Liu-Nguyen). Their headline result, that likelihood pruning saves 5-7x nodes over fixed lists, restates the 1990 T-algorithm versus M-algorithm result. Two things remain open. The **decoding step of primal-BDD and hybrid attacks is the least experimentally validated part of LWE cost estimation**: the lattice estimator models it with a standard-deviation-only Babai probability, a flat d² cost and an explicit "TODO" for the hybrid MitM probability. And **no searched paper puts the exact centred-binomial (CBD) error prior or a box feasibility test inside a BDD list decoder**. The strongest realistic contribution is therefore one paper that measures decoding success against estimator predictions, adds a CBD-aware leaf rule and a validated cost model for relative-threshold list decoders, and ships the bit-exact GPU decoder as an open artifact. That fits a laptop, an fplll pipeline and toy dimensions n ≤ 100. Post it on IACR ePrint first, then target Africacrypt 2027 (deadline 15 January 2027) or IACR Communications in Cryptology. One caveat runs through this whole report. "Not found" means that a few dozen keyword searches over ePrint, arXiv and publisher pages found nothing, with many full texts paywalled. It is not proof that the work has not been done.

## Every VA-ANP ingredient already has a published ancestor

The decoder decomposes into five ingredients, and each one maps onto prior work. **Lindner and Peikert's NearestPlanes takes a fixed vector d of plane counts per Gram-Schmidt level**, chosen offline to maximise min_i d_i·||b*_i|| under a ∏d_i budget. It has success probability ∏ erf(d_i||b*_i||√π/(2s)) and is used explicitly as the decoding step after BKZ in an LWE attack ([Lindner-Peikert, CT-RSA 2011](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~cpeikert/pubs/lwe-analysis.pdf)). VA-ANP's "up to 3 planes per level from ||b*_i|| and sigma" is d_i ∈ {1,2,3}. The per-node rule is a deterministic, thresholded form of Klein's randomized nearest plane. Liu, Ling and Stehlé write Klein's per-level choice as probability proportional to exp(-A·r_ii²·(c_i − x)²), where the centre c_i depends on the target and on coordinates already fixed ([Liu-Ling-Stehlé, IEEE TIT 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.0064)). Keeping planes "within llr·2σ² of the nearest" amounts to keeping those whose Klein weight is within e^(−llr) of the best. Deterministic noise-scaled thresholds on Schnorr-Euchner children also exist in MIMO detection: probabilistic tree pruning ([Shim-Kang, IEEE TSP 2008](https://scholar.korea.ac.kr/handle/2021.sw.korea/122602)) and threshold-based SE enumeration ([Karthikeyan-Saraswady, ICRTIT 2014](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/ICRTIT.2014.6996088)).

The list-pruning rule is the oldest of these. **The T-algorithm "maintains a variable number of paths" adapted to the noise actually encountered and "improves on the M-algorithm"** ([Simmons, IEEE Trans. Commun. 1990](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/26.46522)). K-best variants set the per-layer threshold as the smallest partial distance plus a noise-dependent margin ([Mao-Cheng-Xiang, VTC 2013](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/vtcfall.2013.6692345)) or adapt the pruning threshold inside K-best ([Shen-Eltawil, TCAS-I 2010](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/tcsi.2010.2043017)). On the crypto side, Liu and Nguyen recast NearestPlanes as an instance of enumeration and replaced it with GNR-style pruned enumeration. They report significant improvements over Lindner-Peikert and practicality around dimension 350 ([Liu-Nguyen, CT-RSA 2013](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-36095-4_19)). Kirshanova, May and Wiemer parallelised both strategies ([ePrint 2016/380](https://eprint.iacr.org/2016/380)). Most dangerously for a "likelihood-ranked list" claim, **Luan, Shi, Gu and Zheng ported Aono-Nguyen discrete pruning to BDD, showed that Babai and NearestPlanes are discrete pruning on special sets, and report beating both NearestPlanes and classical pruned enumeration on LWE** ([Symmetry 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-8994/15/2/355); abstract via [OpenAlex](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.3390/sym15020355)). The GPU angle is also taken: a 2020 paper ran Lindner-Peikert-style BDD on a 6 GB GTX 1060, the same hardware class as the team's RTX A1000, and called it the first GPU implementation for LWE ([Esseissah-Bhery-Bahig, IEEE Access 2020](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1109%2FACCESS.2019.2961091)). A 2025 preprint uses a batched Babai nearest plane with GPU reduction for the primal hybrid ([Pulles-Vié, ePrint 2025/1990](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1990)).

The team's own empirical findings fall into the same pattern, with one exception. The 5-7x node saving is the T-versus-M result. The third plane helping at hard settings is Lindner-Peikert's rationale for d_i > 1 where ||b*_i|| is small relative to s. The sphere bound doing nothing follows from the same erf product: factors at large-||b*_i|| levels are essentially 1, so the failure mass, and therefore all useful branching, sits in the last few levels ([LP11](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~cpeikert/pubs/lwe-analysis.pdf)). A relative-to-best threshold already removes what a sphere radius would. The exception is the ablation that **a per-node plane rule adds nothing over plain 3-plane beam search with the same accumulated-metric pruning**. No searched paper states this for BDD. It is a small, publishable negative result, but only as a section of a larger paper, never as the headline.

## The decoding step is the least validated part of LWE cost estimation

The opening is in measurement and modelling, not algorithm design. In the lattice estimator, `primal_bdd` is simply `primal_hybrid(zeta=0, mitm=False, babai=False)`. **The Babai success probability is computed from the error's standard deviation alone, Babai cost is a flat d² operations, and the hybrid MitM success probability is set to 1 under the comment "TODO: the probability in this case needs to be analysed"** ([lattice-estimator lwe_primal.py](https://github.com/malb/lattice-estimator/blob/main/estimator/lwe_primal.py)). Repetitions assume independent trials, and the ζ optimiser is flagged in the code as able to miss minima. Xia et al. state independently that `primal-bdd` "does not take into account some up-to-date results and lacks a thorough theoretical analysis" ([PKC 2024, ePrint 2024/067](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/067)). The estimator also has no option between "Babai" and "SVP in a projected sublattice". The team's list decoders, and Lindner-Peikert, sit in that unmodelled middle.

Two developments make this gap timely. First, **de Perthuis and Trenkić showed that primal estimators using only the first two moments of secret and error cannot distinguish centred distributions of equal variance, and fixed this for uSVP only**. BDD and decoding are not discussed ([PQCrypto 2026, ePrint 2025/2195](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2195)). The decoding-side analogue for CBD errors is unclaimed in the searches. Second, decoding is back at the centre of practical attacks as the inner loop of hybrids. The Fast Slicer hybrid beats the plain primal attack by **up to 5x for n in [160, 210]** but gives no gain for Regev's original parameters ([Karenin et al., ASIACRYPT 2025, ePrint 2025/1910](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1910)). Guess+Verify with batched Babai and BDD dimension reduction beats Cool&Cruel on almost all instances of the Wenger et al. benchmark ([ePrint 2025/1990](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1990)). Bernstein argues that hybrids are exponentially faster than the non-hybrid primal attack and asks what this means for Kyber-512 ([ePrint 2023/1892](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1892)). In a hybrid, the decoder's per-call success probability and cost multiply directly into the attack cost, so a mis-modelled decoder misprices the whole attack. Ducas and Pulles showed how badly decoding-side heuristics can fail, with an unexplained "waterfall-floor" in dual-sieve success ([CRYPTO 2023, ePrint 2023/302](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/302)). An experimental falsification paper of this kind is exactly what a small team can write.

The flip side has to be stated plainly. **The plain primal attack with Kannan embedding and uSVP is still the state of the art for practical LWE records** ([ePrint 2025/1910](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1910)). Record LWE-challenge solves use sieving-based uSVP or two-step progressive BKZ, not a separate decoder ([ePrint 2022/1343](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1343)). A paper that frames list decoding as a better standalone primal finish will be asked why anyone should care. The motivation that holds up is the hybrid inner loop and the estimator's decoding model.

## Six candidate directions, ranked by novelty strength times feasibility

The ranking below weighs novelty strength (how likely a careful reviewer accepts it as new) against feasibility on one laptop with an RTX A1000 6 GB, fplll/fpylll, and n ≤ 100-150. Directions 1-3 combine naturally into one paper. Directions 4 and 5 are either extensions of it or a separate second paper.

| Rank | Direction | Novelty strength | Feasibility | Risk already done |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Decoding-step validation: measured success vs (β, list rule) against estimator predictions, including hybrid assumptions | Medium-high (empirical) | High | Medium: Pulles-Vié and Fast Slicer full texts may contain partial validation |
| 2 | Exact-CBD success model for NearestPlanes and relative-threshold lists, replacing stddev-only `prob_babai` | Medium-high (modelling) | Medium-high | Medium: Luan et al.'s "three probability models" unread |
| 3 | CBD MAP leaf rescoring plus infinity-norm/box feasibility inside the list | Medium | High (cheap code) | Low-medium; benefit uncertain |
| 4 | Batch-CVP GPU beam decoder for hybrid guesses, bit-exact with CPU | Low-medium (implementation) | High (code exists) | Medium-high: Esseissah 2020, Pulles-Vié 2025 |
| 5 | Soft-output list decoder feeding hybrid guessing or DDGR approximate hints | High (if it works) | Low-medium | Low in searches |
| 6 | Best-first (stack/Fano) search with an exact per-level bias term | Medium | Medium | Medium: enumeration literature not fully searched |

Learned pruning and Metropolis-Klein MCMC refinement also turned up in the searches as untried in LWE attacks. Neither is recommended. The BDD tree statistics after BKZ are close to analytic, so a learned rule is unlikely to beat an optimal analytic schedule ([Nguyen-Lee-Dai, arXiv 2010.13481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13481) shows learned gains in MIMO, where that is not the case). Wang and Ling's analysis ties MCMC mixing time to how far the target lies beyond the decoding radius ([arXiv 1704.02673](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.02673)). Both would read as speculative at toy scale.

### 1. Decoding-step validation is the anchor contribution

**Deliverable.** Follow the Postlethwaite-Virdia methodology, which produced full probability mass functions for uSVP success and validated them experimentally ([PKC 2021, ePrint 2020/1308](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/1308)), and apply it to the decoding step. For fixed n ∈ {40, 60, 80, 100}, q = 3329 and CBD η ∈ {2, 3}, run fplll LLL and BKZ-β for β ∈ {10, 15, 20, 25}. Then decode with Babai, Lindner-Peikert with optimised d, the team's relative-threshold beam over a grid of thresholds T, and fpylll pruned CVP enumeration. Record the success rate with Wilson or Clopper-Pearson intervals over at least 100 instances per point, node counts, and wall-clock time. Plot each against the estimator's `primal_bdd` and `primal_hybrid` predictions (including `prob_babai` and `mitm_babai_probability`). Test the independence assumption by re-randomising bases. For a reduced hybrid, measure whether the MitM probability really behaves as the "TODO, set to 1" assumes. Look explicitly for a waterfall-floor-like tail in decoder failure. The n = 80 result (LLL plus a pruned list beating BKZ-10 plus Babai in less time) becomes one cell of a proper (β, T) trade-off surface. It must be re-run with confidence intervals before it is quoted, because it is currently exploratory.

**Compare against.** Lindner-Peikert NearestPlanes, Liu-Nguyen linear pruning (the [cvp-enum code](https://github.com/rub-hgi/cvp-enum) of Kirshanova-May-Wiemer implements both), fpylll CVP enumeration, batched Babai as in [Pulles-Vié](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1990), and, if the full text can be obtained, discrete-pruning BDD ([Luan et al. 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-8994/15/2/355)). Add at least one public instance set: the Darmstadt LWE challenge, whose earlier instances were reportedly generated incorrectly and are being regenerated, so check which cells currently exist ([LWE challenge](https://www.latticechallenge.org/lwe_challenge/challenge.php)), or the Wenger et al. Kyber-like instances ([S&P 2025, ePrint 2024/1229](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1229)), which later papers reuse.

**Risk.** The searches found no systematic measurement of list-decoder success probability versus width on BKZ-reduced LWE bases against estimator predictions. However, the full PDFs of Pulles-Vié, Fast Slicer and Cool+Cruel=Dual ([ePrint 2025/1002](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1002)) were not read, and they are the likeliest places for partial validation. Xia et al. 2024 validated the final-sieve probability in two-step mode, which is adjacent.

### 2. An exact-CBD success and cost model fills a two-moment gap

**Deliverable.** Derive the per-level failure probability of NearestPlanes, and of a relative-threshold list, under the exact distribution of the projected CBD error rather than a Gaussian of equal variance. Validate it against direction 1's data and package it as a drop-in `prob_*` function plus a cost formula for the estimator. Here is a hypothesis to test, not a sourced claim: on q-ary embedding bases after weak reduction, some Gram-Schmidt vectors in the Z-shaped tail stay nearly axis-aligned. The projected error there is close to a single CBD coordinate with support {−2..2}, which is far from Gaussian. That could explain why a third plane matters at hard settings, and it would mean a Gaussian model mis-predicts exactly the levels that dominate failure. Cool+Cruel=Dual already attributes effects to this Z-shape geometry ([ePrint 2025/1002](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1002)), so the idea is plausible and must be cited. In the CLT-dominated middle of the profile the correction will be negligible. The paper should say so and quantify where it is not.

**Compare against.** The estimator's `prob_babai` ([source](https://github.com/malb/lattice-estimator/blob/main/estimator/lwe_primal.py)), Lindner-Peikert's erf product (which they say approximates discrete Gaussians only of width ≥ 6, far wider than CBD(2) ([LP11](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~cpeikert/pubs/lwe-analysis.pdf))), de Perthuis-Trenkić's uSVP-side fix ([ePrint 2025/2195](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2195)), and Xia et al.'s two-step model ([ePrint 2024/067](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/067)).

**Risk.** Luan et al. propose "three probability models" for discrete-pruning BDD on LWE. Their full text was blocked, so whether they model discrete errors is unknown. The exact `prob_babai` formula in the estimator's `prob.py` was also not opened. Read both before writing.

### 3. CBD-aware leaf rescoring is cheap and unclaimed in the searches

**Deliverable.** In an embedding that keeps error and secret coordinates explicit, re-rank the final list by the CBD log-likelihood Σ log P_CBD(e_j) instead of ||e||². Discard any candidate with |e_j| > η, which has probability zero. Where the basis keeps some original coordinates triangular, apply the same infinity-norm test to partially determined coordinates. Then measure success at equal node budget against Euclidean ranking. MIMO precedents are box-constrained integer least squares ([Chang-Han, IEEE TWC 2008](https://manuscript.isc.ac/Inventory/49/1036433.htm)) and infinity-norm sphere decoding ([Seethaler-Bölcskei, IEEE TIT 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.0577v1)). Both must be cited as the origin. Note that BILS does not transfer directly, because unimodular reduction destroys the box on the integer coefficients and the box survives only on the residual.

**Compare against.** Euclidean-ranked beam and LP at equal nodes. Cite Bai-Galbraith's scaled embedding ([ePrint 2013/839](https://eprint.iacr.org/2013/839)), DDGR hint frameworks ([ePrint 2020/292](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/292)), and key enumeration with non-uniform priors ([ePrint 2023/139](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/139)), all of which use the prior outside the tree.

**Risk.** The novelty risk is low-medium, the benefit risk high. The correct vector is usually the shortest candidate anyway, so the gain shows up only at hard settings with near-ties, and it may be small. Report it whatever it turns out to be. A clean null result still answers an unasked question.

### 4. The GPU decoder is an artifact, not a headline

**Deliverable.** Reposition the batched PyTorch decoder for the batch-CVP regime: many targets per reduced basis, one per hybrid guess. Report throughput versus batch size and list width on the 6 GB card against CPU NumPy, fpylll and batched Babai on identical bases. State the bit-exact float64 CPU/GPU equivalence as a reproducibility property, which the searched GPU papers rarely state. Release the code with fixed seeds and the instance generator. The [IACR artifact archive](https://artifacts.iacr.org/asiacrypt/2025/a16/) now hosts such artifacts.

**Compare against.** [Esseissah et al. 2020](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1109%2FACCESS.2019.2961091) (about 6x over sequential LP on a GTX 1060), [Pulles-Vié 2025](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1990), GPU enumeration for fplll ([ePrint 2021/430](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/430)), and MIMO GPU K-best work, where sorting cost limits throughput at large K ([JSPS 2010](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s11265-010-0523-4)).

**Risk.** Medium-high. Whether Pulles-Vié run batched decoding on the GPU, and whether any paper does beam decoding for hybrids, is unverified. On its own this is IEEE-conference grade. As the artifact of the paper built from directions 1-3 it adds credibility.

### 5 and 6. Soft output and best-first search are higher-risk second papers

Soft-output list decoding, meaning per-coordinate reliabilities fed back into guessing or hints, is the most clearly novel idea in the notes. It has the MIMO precedent of the list sphere decoder feeding iterative decoding ([Hochwald-ten Brink, IEEE Trans. Commun. 2003](https://nokia.com/bell-labs/publications-and-media/publications/achieving-near-capacity-on-a-multiple-antenna-channel)), and no crypto paper doing it was found. The concrete experiment is to compute posterior marginals of each s_j over the final list, guess the k least reliable coordinates hybrid-style, and measure the success gain per unit of work against uniform-order guessing. A second variant converts the posteriors into DDGR approximate hints ([leaky-LWE-Estimator](https://github.com/lducas/leaky-LWE-Estimator)) and re-reduces. The risk is engineering scale and the chance that the gain vanishes against a properly tuned hybrid. Best-first stack/Fano search uses the exactly computable per-level noise energy as the bias term. Murugan et al. report that an MMSE-Fano sequential decoder reaches near-ML at low complexity ([IEEE TIT 2006, arXiv cs/0506029](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0506029)). The test is node count at equal success against relative-threshold beam and LN13 pruning. This one carries a real "already done" risk. Best-first and A*-style enumeration may exist in the SVP literature under other names, and the searches did not cover it.

## "Not found" is a search result, not a novelty proof

Every novelty claim above rests on bounded searches. The prior-art pass used about six keyword queries over 2020-2026 ePrint, could not query Google Scholar or dblp, and was blocked from the full texts of Liu-Nguyen 2013, Luan et al. 2023, GNR 2010, Xu et al. 2017 and most IEEE papers. The MIMO cross-check used general web search, not ePrint's own full-text search. A paper doing K-best or "list Babai" decoding for LWE under another name could exist undetected. Before submission the team should do four things. First, obtain and read the full texts of Luan et al., Liu-Nguyen, Pulles-Vié, Fast Slicer, Cool+Cruel=Dual and the [Xu et al. ACNS 2017 BDD study](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-61204-1_13). Second, search ePrint, dblp and Google Scholar for "K-best", "beam search", "stack decoding", "list decoding", "sphere decoding", "MAP decoding" and "soft-output" combined with "LWE" or "BDD", plus "centered binomial" with "nearest plane". Third, check Albrecht-Heninger's BDD-with-predicate work (EUROCRYPT 2021), which was not verified. Fourth, ask one lattice cryptanalyst, for instance on the fplll-devel list, whether the decoding-validation study is already in progress somewhere. The 2025 papers show active groups (CWI, Bochum, Télécom Paris) working on exactly this inner loop, so the window may be short.

The framing matters as much as the content. The paper should present VA-ANP honestly as "a T-algorithm / threshold K-best decoder, imported from MIMO detection, used as the BDD step", credit the communications origin, and place the contribution in what was measured and modelled.

## Conclusion

The VA-ANP episode is useful evidence, not a failure. Every "improvement" the team designed collapsed onto a known algorithm. Their experiments still reproduced known decoding effects on real LWE bases, and found one clean ablation. That is exactly the capability the decoding step lacks. Two moves would turn it into a contribution: make the estimator's assumptions the object under test, and make the exact CBD distribution the modelling lever. The estimator's stddev-only Babai probability and its open hybrid TODO are where the field's numbers are least checked, and they are now load-bearing in hybrid attacks.

The broader lesson for a student team is that in mature cryptanalysis, novelty at this scale comes from falsifiable measurement with open artifacts more often than from new search heuristics. Ducas-Pulles and Postlethwaite-Virdia are the models to follow, and the competition from 2025 onward means speed of posting matters. If direction 1 shows the estimator's decoding predictions are accurate even at CBD(2), that is a publishable and useful result too. If it shows they are off, the CBD model in direction 2 is the paper's second half.
