# GPU/parallel decoding, public LWE benchmarks, and venues for a student lattice-cryptanalysis contribution

Research date: 2026-11-07. Verification legend: [V] = page opened (ePrint/official page fetched) or bibliographic data confirmed by search on the primary record; [S] = confirmed only through search-result snippets or secondary listings; [U] = from background knowledge, not verified in this session. Treat [U] items as leads to check before citing.

## Q1. Existing GPU/parallel work on enumeration, sieving and decoding. Is a GPU batched list decoder for the primal attack already published?

### Takeaway
GPU enumeration (2010-2021) and GPU sieving (G6K-GPU, 2021) are established. GPU BDD decoding for LWE is also not new. A 2020 IEEE Access paper already ran Lindner-Peikert-style BDD enumeration on a 6 GB GTX 1060, and a 2025 ePrint (Pulles-Vié) does batched Babai nearest-plane for the primal hybrid attack with GPU lattice reduction. "First GPU BDD decoder for LWE" is therefore not a defensible novelty claim. What remains open is a **batched beam/list (K-best-style) decoder whose output is bit-identical to the CPU version, run on consumer hardware, with measured success-probability-vs-width curves compared against estimator predictions.** That is a narrower but plausible implementation/empirical contribution.

### Cited Findings
**GPU enumeration (SVP)**
- [S] Hermans, Schneider, Buchmann, Vercauteren, Preneel, "Parallel Shortest Lattice Vector Enumeration on Graphics Cards", AFRICACRYPT 2010, LNCS 6055 (ePrint 2009/601). Reported about 5x faster in high dimensions. — [Springer](https://rd.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-12678-9_4); [ePrint 2009/601](https://eprint.iacr.org/2009/601)
- [S] Kuo, Schneider, Dagdelen, Reichelt, Buchmann, Cheng, Yang, "Extreme Enumeration on GPU and in Clouds: How Many Dollars You Need to Break SVP Challenges", CHES 2011, LNCS 6917, pp. 176-191. GPU plus MapReduce extreme pruning; dimension-114 SVP in under 2 days on 8 NVIDIA GPUs; first place at dimension 120; talk slides say one GTX 480 was about 12x one i7 core; thesis cites about USD 2300 of Amazon rental for dim 120. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-23951-9_12); [dblp](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/ches/KuoSDRBCY11); [slides](https://iacr.org/workshops/ches/ches2011/presentations/Session%204/CHES2011_Session4_2.pdf)
- [V] Pohmann, Stevens, Zumbrägel, "Lattice Enumeration on GPUs for fplll", ePrint 2021/430. CUDA KFP enumeration, compatible with fplll's external-enumerator interface, faster than multithreaded CPU enumeration in higher dimensions. Code: fplll-cuda-enumeration (GitHub, FeanorTheElf). — [ePrint 2021/430](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/430); [fplll-devel post](https://groups.google.com/g/fplll-devel/c/TwdE0Cnt4qU)

**GPU sieving / reduction**
- [V] Ducas, Stevens, van Woerden, "Advanced Lattice Sieving on GPUs, with Tensor Cores", EUROCRYPT 2021 (ePrint 2021/141). G6K-GPU with a "dual-hash" technique. SVP Darmstadt challenge dimension 180 (previous record 155) in 51.6 days on 4 NVIDIA Turing GPUs with 1.5 TB RAM. Authors claim about two orders of magnitude gain in wall-clock time and energy. — [ePrint 2021/141](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/141); [EUROCRYPT archive PDF](https://iacr.org/archive/eurocrypt2021/126960219/126960219.pdf)
- [S] Zhao, Ding, Yang (TCHES 2025): solved the 183-dimensional Darmstadt SVP challenge in 30 days on a 112-core CPU server with 0.87 TB RAM, by optimising sieving memory access. This was a CPU result. — [TCHES download](https://tches.iacr.org/index.php/TCHES/article/download/12051/11895/13908) (from search snippet; exact title not confirmed)
- [V/S] Ducas, Pulles, Stevens, "Towards a Modern LLL Implementation", ASIACRYPT 2025 (ePrint 2025/774). BLASter is about 1000 lines of C++/Python. It is >10x faster than flatter/OptLLL and 100x faster than fplll in dimensions 256-1024. The artifact is in the IACR artifact archive. — [ePrint 2025/774](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/774); [IACR artifact](https://artifacts.iacr.org/asiacrypt/2025/a16/)

**GPU / parallel BDD decoding for LWE (closest prior work to the student's decoder)**
- [V] **Esseissah, Bhery, Bahig, "Improving BDD Enumeration for LWE Problem Using GPU", IEEE Access 8:19737-19749, 2020, DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2019.2961091.** GPU-parallel BDD (Lindner-Peikert style). Measured on an **NVIDIA GTX 1060 6 GB**: about 6x over sequential Lindner-Peikert BDD and about 9x over Kirshanova's pruned enumeration. The authors claim it is the first GPU implementation for LWE. — [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1109%2FACCESS.2019.2961091); [ASU repository](https://research.asu.edu.eg/handle/123456789/187785); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/338147155_Improving_BDD_Enumeration_for_LWE_Problem_Using_GPU)
- [S] "Parallel Implementation of BDD enumeration for LWE", ePrint 2016/380: multi-core CPU parallel BDD enumeration. [U] Authors Kirshanova, May, Wiemer, ACNS 2016. This is the baseline Esseissah et al. compare against. — [ePrint 2016/380](https://eprint.iacr.org/2016/380)
- [V] **Pulles (CWI), Vié (Télécom Paris), "Accelerating the Primal Hybrid Attack against Sparse LWE using GPUs", ePrint 2025/1990 (Oct 2025, category Implementation, preprint).** Contributions: cuBLASter (GPU BLASter, which beats BLASter at dim ≥512), dimension reduction of the BDD instance, a **batched variant of Babai's Nearest Plane** for Guess+Verify, and GPU G6K integrated into cuBLASter's BKZ. On the Wenger et al. S&P'25 benchmark it reports higher success rates than Cool&Cruel on almost all instances. The abstract page does not state whether batched Babai itself runs on the GPU, and it mentions no beam/list decoding. The full PDF needs checking. — [ePrint 2025/1990](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1990)
- [V] Karenin, Kirshanova, May, Nowakowski, "Fast Slicer for Batch-CVP: Making Lattice Hybrid Attacks Practical", ASIACRYPT 2025 (ePrint 2025/1910). First efficient Randomized-Slicer implementation with LSH for batch-CVP. The BDD-hybrid beats the plain primal attack by up to 5x for n in [160,210] (ternary and CBD secrets). For Regev's original parameters the hybrid "cannot improve over primal". The abstract does not mention GPUs. — [ePrint 2025/1910](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1910)

**MIMO K-best / sphere decoding on GPU (the same algorithmic family as beam-search BDD)**
- [S] Several GPU K-Best and fixed-complexity sphere decoder implementations exist. A 2024 *Electronics* paper reports up to 5x latency/bit and 120x energy/bit over CPU for 4/16/64-QAM. A J. Signal Processing Systems 2010 paper argues that K-Best sorting cost limits GPU throughput at large K. An ICAE paper gives GPU FSD meeting LTE real-time rates. — [Electronics 2024 record](https://harvester.ues.edu.sv/vufind/ProQuestFSGRecord/3084744009?lng=da); [JSPS 2010](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s11265-010-0523-4); [ICAE](https://content.iospress.com/articles/integrated-computer-aided-engineering/ica00410); [UPV thesis (Roger)](https://theses.eurasip.org/document/design-and-implementation-of-efficient-algorithms-for-wireless-mimo-communication-systems/)

### Inferences
- The student's batched beam-search BDD is algorithmically a K-best decoder on an LLL/BKZ-reduced LWE basis. GPU K-best is established in MIMO (dimensions 4-64, small alphabets), and GPU BDD for LWE exists (Esseissah et al. 2020, same 6 GB GPU class). A paper must cite both and position against them.
- Residual novelty angles that the sources above do not cover:
  1. **Batching across many targets per basis** (many LWE samples or hybrid guesses sharing one reduced basis). This is the batch-CVP regime of 2025/1910 and 2025/1990, but done with a beam/list decoder instead of the Slicer or plain Babai.
  2. **Bit-exact CPU/GPU equivalence in float64** as a reproducibility property. This is rarely stated in the GPU papers above.
  3. **Measured success probability vs beam width vs BKZ block size**, i.e. a cost/success trade-off curve for the final decoding step, compared with lattice-estimator predictions (see Q3).
- A head-to-head against fplll's CVP/enumeration (`fpylll.CVP`, pruned BDD enumeration) and against batched Babai (as in 2025/1990) is the minimum baseline set a reviewer will expect.

### Gaps
- Could not open the 2025/1990 full PDF. Unknown: whether batched Babai runs on GPU, the hardware, and whether code is public.
- Did not find a published **GPU beam-search/K-best decoder specifically for the LWE primal or hybrid attack**. "None found" is not proof of absence. Searches used ePrint and general web only, not IEEE Xplore full-text.
- Hermans et al. venue/volume and Kirshanova-May-Wiemer authorship were not confirmed on primary pages.

## Q2. Public benchmarks: LWE challenge records and methods, SVP challenge, Kyber toy instances, the lattice estimator

### Takeaway
The TU Darmstadt LWE challenge (latticechallenge.org/lwe_challenge) is active. The newest records come in 2025-2026 from Jintai Ding's and Shi Bai/Yu Yu's groups, and the largest solved is n = 100 at α = 0.005 (Aug 2026). The hall-of-fame page lists no methods. Published methods for the record holders are primal uSVP via G6K/G6K-GPU sieving or progressive BKZ + final sieve ("two-step mode"), not a separate BDD decoding step. Toy instances (n = 2, 5, 10) and n = 40-120 are available, so these instances are a natural public testbed. The site also notes that earlier instances were generated incorrectly and are being regenerated.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Challenge definition: recover s from (A, b = As + e mod q), A and s uniform, e Gaussian with σ, relative error α = σ/q. Hardness depends mainly on α and n. Toy challenges: n = 2 (α 0.005), n = 5 (α 0.010), n = 10 (α 0.015). Standard grid: n = 40...120 step 5, α = 0.005...0.070 step 0.005. The page states earlier instances were "generated incorrectly" and unbreakable, were removed, and the protocol is being rerun, with some instances still grey (not generated). q is not given on the overview page. — [LWE challenge](https://www.latticechallenge.org/lwe_challenge/challenge.php)
- [V] Hall of fame (45 entries as of 2026-11-07). Hardest by n at α = 0.005: n = 100 (Jintai Ding, 2026-08-10), n = 95 (Jinghui Wang, Changhong Xu, Honggang Hu, Yiming Gao, 2024-09-22), n = 90 (Xia, L. Wang, G. Wang, Gu, B. - [V] Wenger, Saxena, Malhou, Thieu, Lauter, "Benchmarking Attacks on Learning with Errors", IEEE S&P 2025 (ePrint 2024/1229). MitM solved decision-LWE up to h = 4 in under 1 h. **uSVP recovered no secrets after more than 1100 h.** They say code is released (URL not on the abstract page). — [ePrint 2024/1229](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1229)

- [S] Lattice estimator: the primal attack is modelled as uSVP and as primal-BDD (and hybrid) in the docs. — [lattice-estimator docs, LWE primal](https://lattice-estimator.readthedocs.io/en/latest/algorithms/lwe-primal.html). [U] Repository github.com/malb/lattice-estimator; it originates from Albrecht, Player, Scott, "On the concrete hardness of Learning with Errors", J. Math. Cryptol. 2015.

### Inferences
- The student's setting (n ≤ 100, q = 3329) is **not** the Darmstadt parameterisation, where q and α vary per instance. To claim a benchmark result they should run on official challenge instances (or the Wenger et al. instances) as well as their own q = 3329 instances.
- Solving the small, already-solved Darmstadt instances (e.g. n = 40-55 at high α) on a laptop GPU is a credible reproducibility demonstration. It is not a record, because records at n ≥ 85 needed sieving at scale.
- Because the Darmstadt instances were reportedly regenerated, some previously listed (n, α) cells may be unsolved under the new protocol. Check the grid for green vs grey cells before claiming "first solve".

### Gaps
- Per-entry "Details" pages (method, hardware, time) were not opened, so it is unverified how the 2024-2026 records (Ding; Wang-Xu-Hu-Gao; Bai et al.) were solved, and whether any used an explicit BDD/decoding step.
- No current Darmstadt SVP challenge record for 2026 was confirmed. The latest found is 183 (Zhao-Ding-Yang, TCHES 2025, from a search snippet).
- q per LWE-challenge instance was not captured.

## Q3. Empirical papers that validate attack estimates experimentally: methodology and open estimate-vs-experiment gaps

### Takeaway
The standard methodology is to generate many random LWE instances at small n, run (progressive) BKZ/sieving, record the success block size or probability distribution, and compare with a simulator (GSA/BKZ simulator, CN11) and the lattice estimator. Mature results exist for the uSVP/primal step (AGVW17, Postlethwaite-Virdia PKC 2021, two-step mode PKC 2024) and for dual-sieve (Ducas-Pulles CRYPTO 2023). The **decoding step of primal-BDD and hybrid attacks (Babai, list/beam, slicer success probability vs cost) is much less validated experimentally**, apart from the 2025 batch-CVP/hybrid papers.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Postlethwaite, Virdia, "On the Success Probability of Solving Unique SVP via BKZ", PKC 2021, LNCS 12710, pp. 68-98 (ePrint 2020/1308). Extends the success-probability simulator to BKZ 2.0 and Progressive BKZ and outputs **full probability mass functions** of the successful block size. Extensive experiments on uSVP lattices from LWE with Gaussian, binary and ternary secrets. Explains why the AGVW17 attacks succeeded at smaller block sizes than expected. Simulating Kyber1024 takes 31 s versus 2 h for DDGR20. — [ePrint 2020/1308](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/1308); [PKC archive PDF](https://www.iacr.org/archive/pkc2021/127110144/127110144.pdf)
- [U] Albrecht, Göpfert, Virdia, Wunderer, "Revisiting the Expected Cost of Solving uSVP and Applications to LWE", ASIACRYPT 2017 (ePrint 2017/815). Experimentally validated the "2016 estimate" for uSVP success. Cited by Postlethwaite-Virdia as the experiments they explain.
- [S] Dachman-Soled, Ducas, Gong, Rossi, "LWE with Side Information: Attacks and Concrete Security Estimation", CRYPTO 2020 (ePrint 2020/292). Leaky-LWE framework and estimator with a primal-attack embedding. — [ePrint 2020/292](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/292.pdf)
- [V] Ducas, Pulles, "Does the Dual-Sieve Attack on Learning with Errors even Work?", CRYPTO 2023, LNCS 14083, pp. 37-69 (ePrint 2023/302). Experiments contradict the heuristics and show a "waterfall-floor" phenomenon like LDPC decoding failures. Conclusion: dual-sieve-FFT success probabilities are "presumably significantly overestimated". Follow-up: "Accurate Score Prediction for Dual-Sieve Attacks" (ePrint 2023/1850) estimates false positives and false negatives accurately. — [ePrint 2023/302](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/302); [ePrint 2023/1850](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1850)
- [S] Xia, Wang et al., PKC 2024 / ePrint 2024/067: a refined estimator that includes the success probability of the final sieve in two-step mode, validated on Darmstadt instances. — [ePrint 2024/067](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/067)
- [V] Karenin-Kirshanova-May-Nowakowski (ASIACRYPT 2025): an experimental BDD-hybrid with Slicer batch-CVP. Measured up to 5x over primal for n in [160, 210]. Theory gives cost T^(1-K) with K ≈ (1 + H(D)/0.058)^(-1), and K "can be small", so practical gains are limited. — [ePrint 2025/1910](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1910)
- [V] Wenger et al. S&P 2025 compare measured attack performance with theoretical estimates for standardized-like parameters. uSVP failed after more than 1100 h on their Kyber-like sparse instances, where ML-style and MitM attacks succeeded. — [ePrint 2024/1229](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/1229)
- [V] Pulles-Vié 2025: "no efficient open-source implementation" of the SAC'19 Guess+Verify hybrid attack existed before their work. This is direct evidence that the implementation of decoding-based hybrid attacks is under-served. — [ePrint 2025/1990](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1990)

### Inferences
- A student-feasible empirical contribution would follow the Postlethwaite-Virdia methodology but for the **decoding step**. For fixed n, q = 3329 and a fixed error distribution, sweep BKZ block size β and beam width K, then measure (a) the decoding success probability, (b) wall-clock on CPU vs GPU, and (c) the lattice-estimator primal-BDD / hybrid prediction. Report pmfs or confidence intervals over ≥100 instances per point.
- The "waterfall-floor" observation in dual attacks hints that decoders can show non-Gaussian failure tails. Checking whether beam-search BDD on reduced bases has a similar floor is a concrete, novel experimental question. This is an inference, not a published claim.

### Gaps
- Did not find a paper that systematically measures **list/beam BDD success probability vs width on BKZ-reduced LWE bases** against estimator predictions. This is possibly a real gap, but full-text search was not done.
- AGVW17 details are from memory; they were not re-fetched.

## Q4. Realistic venues and formats; what reviewers expect

### Takeaway
For a student result, realistic targets are, first, an IACR ePrint preprint (no review; establishes priority), and then one of IACR Communications in Cryptology (quarterly cycles), Africacrypt 2027 (deadline 15 Jan 2027), PQCrypto 2027 (deadline 17 Nov 2026), Indocrypt 2027, ACNS 2027 workshops, or SPACE. TCHES/CHES is the implementation venue, but it is a high bar for a toy-dimension result. Reviewers in this area expect open code, reproducibility, and comparisons against fplll/fpylll, G6K (or G6K-GPU) and the lattice estimator.

## Q5. Student-scale or small-team papers that contributed via careful experiments or implementation

### Takeaway
Several impactful lattice-cryptanalysis papers are essentially implementation or experiment papers by 2-5 authors, often with a PhD student lead: GPU enumeration for fplll (largely a student's code: Pohmann), BLASter, the Pulles-Vié GPU hybrid, Ducas-Pulles dual-sieve experiments, and the earlier Japanese university LWE-challenge solves. The bar is a clear, reproducible measurement with open code, not record dimensions.

### Cited Findings
- [V] Pohmann-Stevens-Zumbrägel (ePrint 2021/430): the CUDA enumerator was written by Simon Pohmann and offered to the fplll project as a library on the fplll-devel list (Dec 2020). — [ePrint 2021/430](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/430); [fplll-devel](https://groups.google.com/g/fplll-devel/c/TwdE0Cnt4qU)
- [V] Pulles (PhD at CWI) and Vié (Télécom Paris): a two-author implementation paper whose stated motivation is the lack of open-source G+V hybrid implementations. — [ePrint 2025/1990](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1990)
- [V] Ducas-Pulles CRYPTO 2023: a two-author paper whose main contribution is experiments falsifying heuristics. — [ePrint 2023/302](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/302)
- [V] Esseissah-Bhery-Bahig (Ain Shams University), IEEE Access 2020: a three-author GPU BDD paper on a consumer GTX 1060 6 GB. This is the closest analogue to the student project, both in hardware and in topic. — [ASU repository](https://research.asu.edu.eg/handle/123456789/187785)
- [V] University teams solved early LWE-challenge cells with modest resources (e.g. Xu, Fukushima, Kiyomoto, Takagi, 2016, n = 40-60 at α = 0.005-0.015; Kashiwabara-Teruya, 2017). — [Hall of fame](https://www.latticechallenge.org/lwe_challenge/halloffame.php)
- [V] Xia, Wang et al. (Shanghai Jiao Tong / Xidian groups) turned strategy-selection experiments plus open code (pro-pnj-bkz) into PKC 2024 and several LWE-challenge records. — [ePrint 2022/1343](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/1343); [ePrint 2024/067](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/067)

### Inferences
- The most credible student framing is to **position the work as an open, reproducible GPU beam/K-best BDD decoder for the batch-CVP regime of primal and hybrid attacks.** It would come with (i) exact CPU equivalence, (ii) speedups vs NumPy/fplll/batched Babai on the same reduced bases, (iii) measured success probability vs (β, K) against lattice-estimator primal-BDD and hybrid predictions, and (iv) validation on official Darmstadt and/or Wenger et al. instances. Claiming "first GPU BDD for LWE" would be rejected given Esseissah et al. 2020 and Pulles-Vié 2025.
- Contribution (iii), an empirical estimate-vs-experiment study of the decoding step, has the strongest novelty potential. The speedup alone (ii) is likely only workshop/IEEE-conference grade.
