# MIMO detection / wireless lattice decoding techniques as candidates for BDD in LWE / ML-KEM primal attacks

Scope note: research done 2026-11-07. A reference is marked **[opened]** if its page or PDF was opened in this session, **[index-verified]** if only a primary index (arXiv, ePrint, dblp, IEEE metadata, university repository) was seen in search results (title, authors, venue matched), and **[UNVERIFIED]** if it comes from background knowledge and no page was seen. Crypto-usage claims ("not found") mean: targeted searches of the web and ePrint did not surface such a paper. That is evidence of absence, not proof. Aggregator sites (bohrium, typeset, emergentmind, etc.) were not used as sources.

Mapping used throughout: MIMO model y = Hx + n with QR(H) = R gives a tree over the integer symbols x, processed from the last row of R. In the LWE primal BDD view, t = v + e with v in L(B). Babai/nearest-plane over a reduced basis B is the same tree: level i has "SNR" ||b*_i|| / sigma_e. After BKZ the profile decays geometrically (GSA), so the *first-decided* levels (smallest ||b*_i||, i.e. last GS indices) are the unreliable ones. In MIMO language, this is a channel with a very poorly conditioned R whose weakest streams are decided first, and whose strong streams are essentially noiseless.

## Q1. Detection ordering and preprocessing (V-BLAST/SQRD, LR-aided detection, MMSE-regularized lattice decoding)

### Takeaway
The main MIMO preprocessing tools either already exist in lattice cryptanalysis or are dominated by BKZ. LR-aided detection is BKZ. MMSE augmentation of the channel matrix is structurally the Kannan/Bai-Galbraith embedding with secret coordinates and scaling. Column reordering after reduction is provably harmful to Babai in the analogous ILS setting. The one MIMO preprocessing idea with no crypto analogue found is the MMSE-DFE "biased" (non-zero-forcing) decision metric inside the tree, from Murugan-El Gamal-Damen-Caire. For ML-KEM, though, it reduces to an embedding scaling that is already ~1.

### Cited Findings
- **Murugan, El Gamal, Damen, Caire, "A Unified Framework for Tree Search Decoding: Rediscovering the Sequential Decoder," IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 52(3), 2006** [index-verified: arXiv cs/0506029, EURECOM page]. The paper splits closest-lattice-point search into preprocessing and tree search. Preprocessing has three parts: left (MMSE-DFE filtering), right (lattice reduction plus column permutation) and tree formation (QR). The search is a branch-and-bound framework that contains all sphere decoders as special cases. Its preferred algorithm resembles the Fano sequential decoder ("MMSE-Fano"), and the MMSE-DFE lattice decoder performs close to ML at much lower complexity. — [arXiv cs/0506029](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0506029); [EURECOM record](https://www.eurecom.edu/en/publication/4148)
- **Wübben, Böhnke, Kühn, Kammeyer, "MMSE Extension of V-BLAST based on Sorted QR Decomposition," IEEE VTC2003-Fall, pp. 508-512** (DOI 10.1109/VETECF.2003.1285069) [index-verified]. MMSE-SQRD uses a sorted QR of the (augmented) channel matrix and reaches V-BLAST error performance at a fraction of the cost. — [Bremen PDF](https://www.ant.uni-bremen.de/sixcms/media.php/102/4432/VTC_Fall_2003_wuebben.pdf)
- **Chang, Wen, Xie, "Effects of the LLL Reduction on the Success Probability of the Babai Point and on the Complexity of Sphere Decoding," IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 59(8):4915-4926, 2013** [index-verified: arXiv 1204.2009]. The paper proves that LLL reduction always raises the Babai point's success probability, and that the SQRD and V-BLAST column orderings, unlike LLL's own permutations, *may lower* it. It also proves LLL lowers sphere-decoder node count, and that Babai success probability is a sharper lower bound on ILS success than the Hassibi-Boyd bound. — [arXiv 1204.2009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.2009); [author PDF](https://www.cs.mcgill.ca/~chang/pub/ChaWX13.pdf)
- **Bai, Galbraith, "Lattice Decoding Attacks on Binary LWE," ACISP 2014, LNCS 8544, pp. 322-337** [index-verified: ePrint 2013/839]. The attack recasts binary-LWE as inhomogeneous SIS, rescales the lattice so that secret and error coordinates have balanced size, and then solves CVP. This is the crypto version of variance-balancing a regularized/augmented system. — [ePrint 2013/839](https://eprint.iacr.org/2013/839)
- **Lindner, Peikert, "Better Key Sizes (and Attacks) for LWE-Based Encryption," CT-RSA 2011** [opened, PDF]. The authors point out that in a BKZ-reduced basis the last Gram-Schmidt vectors are short, so the Babai parallelepiped is "long and skinny" and Babai fails. Their fix enlarges it per level, not by reordering. — [PDF](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~cpeikert/pubs/lwe-analysis.pdf)
- Survey context: **Yang, Hanzo, "Fifty Years of MIMO Detection: The Road to Large-Scale MIMOs," IEEE Commun. Surveys & Tutorials 17(4):1941-1988, 2015** [index-verified]. — [arXiv 1507.05138](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.05138v1); [Southampton eprint](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/367947)
- [UNVERIFIED] Element-based / complex LLL LR-aided detection: Gan, Ling, Mow, "Complex lattice reduction algorithm for low-complexity full-diversity MIMO detection," IEEE TSP 2009. Zhou, Ma, "Element-based lattice reduction algorithms for large MIMO detection," IEEE JSAC 2013. These were not opened. For the LWE use case both are weaker than BKZ.

### Inferences
- **MMSE regularization ≈ Bai-Galbraith/Kannan embedding.** The MMSE trick augments H to [H; (sigma_n/sigma_x) I] and decodes in that lattice. In LWE, the primal embedding with an identity block for s (secret coordinates part of the short vector) has the same structure, and the Bai-Galbraith scaling factor plays the role of sigma_n/sigma_x. For ML-KEM, secret and error in the key-generation equation are both drawn from CBD(eta1), so the balancing factor is ~1 and MMSE-style weighting adds nothing beyond the standard embedding. This parameter fact is from FIPS 203 background knowledge and was not re-verified here. Not novel.
- **MMSE-DFE bias inside the tree** (Murugan et al.) is the one preprocessing idea that is not just a lattice change. The MMSE-DFE feedforward filter makes the per-level metric *biased*, trading a small bias for much lower effective noise at weak levels, and that is what makes their Fano/stack search cheap. In the LWE case the "symbols" (basis coefficients) are unbounded integers with no prior. MMSE regularization of unbounded integer coordinates is meaningless unless the coordinates that carry the prior (s and e) are kept explicit. They are kept explicit in the embedding, so this collapses to the previous point. Low novelty potential.
- **Column reordering after BKZ is likely harmful or neutral.** Chang-Wen-Xie show SQRD/V-BLAST orderings can *decrease* Babai success relative to LLL's own permutation. BKZ is a stronger ordering-plus-reduction than SQRD. Re-sorting a BKZ basis would break size-reduction and the GSA profile. This is consistent with the team's observation that their own variance-aware plane rule adds nothing.
- **Why the team's variance-aware rule likely gave nothing.** Lindner-Peikert's success formula is a product over levels of erf(d_i ||b*_i|| sqrt(pi) / (2s)). Factors at high-||b*_i|| levels are ~1, so all the failure probability sits in the last few levels. Any sensible rule, K-best included, already spends its branches there. A "variance-aware" reallocation can only shuffle mass among those few levels.

### Gaps
- No crypto paper was found that tests MMSE-DFE-biased metrics or SQRD-type orderings on BKZ-reduced LWE bases. Given the inferences above, a negative result is expected.
- Gan-Ling-Mow and Zhou-Ma were not opened (UNVERIFIED).

## Q2. Tree-search variants (K-best/adaptive K, probabilistic and statistical pruning, FSD, best-first/stack/Fano/A*, Schnorr-Euchner with radius update, list/soft-output SD)

### Takeaway
Several of the team's ingredients have direct crypto counterparts: fixed per-level branching (FSD) is Lindner-Peikert NearestPlanes, and probabilistic or likelihood pruning is GNR/Liu-Nguyen pruned BDD enumeration. Explicit K-best/M-algorithm breadth-first search with a survivor list, best-first (stack/Fano/A*) search, and soft-output/list sphere decoding were **not found** in any LWE cryptanalysis paper. Of these, best-first search with an MMSE-Fano-style metric and soft-output reliabilities feeding a hybrid/hint stage look like the most plausible novelty.

### Cited Findings

Proposes K-best Schnorr-Euchner (KSE) and a soft-output modified KSE (MKSE), with VLSI chips at 53.3 Mb/s (hard) and >100 Mb/s (soft). — [Lund record](https://lup.lub.lu.se/search/publication/415182); [dblp](https://dblp.dagstuhl.de/rec/journals/jsac/GuoN06.html)
- **Shim, Kang, "Sphere decoding with a probabilistic tree pruning," IEEE TSP 56(10):4867-4878, 2008** (DOI 10.1109/TSP.2008.923808) [index-verified: Korea Univ. repository]. Adds a probabilistic constraint to the sphere constraint so that unlikely branches are dropped early. A single pruning probability sets the complexity/performance trade-off, and the authors report a large complexity reduction with negligible loss. — [Korea Univ. record](https://scholar.korea.ac.kr/handle/2021.sw.korea/122602)
- **Gowaikar, Hassibi, "Statistical Pruning for Near-Maximum Likelihood Decoding," IEEE TSP 55(6):2661-2675, 2007** (DOI 10.1109/TSP.2006.890912) [index-verified: Caltech record]. - **Barbero, Thompson, "Fixing the Complexity of the Sphere Decoder for MIMO Detection," IEEE TWC 7(6):2131-2142, 2008** (DOI 10.1109/TWC.2008.060378) [index-verified: dblp, Edinburgh repository]. - **Lindner-Peikert NearestPlanes (CT-RSA 2011)** [opened]. Recurses on d_i ≥ 1 nearest planes at level i, with d_i chosen to maximize min_i(d_i·||b*_i||). Footnote 4 notes that Klein's algorithm also recurses on several planes, but probabilistically. Footnote 5 suggests searching a "ball made of bricks" (copies of the parallelepiped), which was left as future work. — [PDF](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~cpeikert/pubs/lwe-analysis.pdf)
- **Liu, Nguyen, "Solving BDD by Enumeration: An Update," CT-RSA 2013, LNCS 7779** [index-verified: Springer DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-36095-4_19]. Applies pruned enumeration (GNR extreme pruning) to BDD, improves on Lindner-Peikert, and reports that BDD enumeration can be practical around dimension 350. — [Springer/unpaywall](https://unpaywall.org/10.1007%2F978-3-642-36095-4_19)
- **Gama, Nguyen, Regev, "Lattice Enumeration Using Extreme Pruning," EUROCRYPT 2010, LNCS 6110, pp. 257-278** [index-verified: dblp, Springer]. Gives a sound analysis of pruning and an asymptotic speedup of about 1.414^n over basic enumeration. — [Springer PDF](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-3-642-13190-5_13.pdf); [dblp](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/html/conf/eurocrypt/GamaNR10)
- **Aono, Nguyen, "Random Sampling Revisited: Lattice Enumeration with Discrete Pruning," EUROCRYPT 2017** [index-verified: ePrint 2017/155]. Discrete pruning generalizes Schnorr random sampling. The page lists a related IEEE TCOM 2024 journal reference. No mention of cryptography. — [arXiv 2512.11195](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11195)

### Inferences
- **The team's decoder is essentially in the crypto literature already.** Fixed per-level branching is Lindner-Peikert, and FSD is structurally the same algorithm: full expansion at the first, least reliable levels, which after BKZ are the last GS indices where Babai's recursion starts. Likelihood/T-algorithm pruning plays the role of GNR/Liu-Nguyen pruning bounds and Shim-Kang probabilistic pruning. "A third candidate per level helps at hard settings" corresponds to d_i = 3 at the weak levels in Lindner-Peikert. A breadth-first, survivor-list (K-best/M-algorithm) formulation with *global* metric sorting across siblings is not literally in Lindner-Peikert (fixed product tree) or Liu-Nguyen (DFS with pruning). It is a modest but legitimate difference to state precisely.
- **Most promising tree-search novelty: best-first / stack / Fano with an MMSE-style or bias-corrected metric.** Murugan et al. report that the Fano-type sequential decoder with MMSE-DFE preprocessing reaches near-ML at very low complexity. Plain best-first by partial distance is biased toward shallow nodes, and the Fano bias term corrects this by subtracting the expected per-level noise energy (~sigma²) at each level. For an LWE BDD tree with a known per-coordinate error variance, that bias term is computable exactly. No crypto use was found.
- **Adaptive K.** "Sphere Decoding Revisited" gives a nK node bound with a single deviation parameter. Its radius/K schedule could be calibrated to the GSA profile, which would give a predictable cost model of the kind estimators like. Not found in crypto.
- **Soft output / list decoding.** Per-coordinate reliabilities (LLR-like) of the recovered e or s from a list sphere decoder could feed a second stage, for example:
  - hybrid guessing (Howgrave-Graham style), by guessing the least reliable coordinates first
  - "approximate hints" in the Dachman-Soled-Ducas-Gong-Rossi framework (see Q5)

  This gives an iterative "turbo" loop between the lattice decoder and a combinatorial or statistical stage. No such crypto paper was found. This is the most speculative idea, and also the most clearly novel.

### Gaps
- The Agrell et al. 2002, Damen-El Gamal-Caire 2003 and Hassibi-Vikalo 2005 entries were not opened (UNVERIFIED), and neither was the Studer et al. JSAC 2008 journal version.
- Liu-Nguyen's exact pruning functions for BDD and their relation to Shim-Kang's probabilistic constraint were not checked in full text.
- No ePrint paper was found that runs a breadth-first K-best decoder on LWE. The ePrint full-text search was not run directly, so this rests on web search coverage.

## Q3. Sampling-based decoders (Klein, decoding by sampling, MCMC/Gibbs)

### Takeaway
Klein-style randomized nearest plane is well known to cryptographers: it underlies GPV sampling, Lindner-Peikert cite it, and Stehlé co-authored the "decoding by sampling" paper. MCMC lattice decoding (Wang-Ling) is even co-posted on ePrint with a BDD analysis. However, no paper was found that *uses* Klein sampling, Metropolis-Klein MCMC, or Gibbs sampling as the BDD stage of an LWE primal attack at cryptographic dimension.

### Cited Findings
- **Liu, Ling, Stehlé, "Decoding by Sampling: A Randomized Lattice Algorithm for Bounded Distance Decoding," IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 57(9):5933-5945, 2011** (DOI 10.1109/TIT.2011.2162180) [index-verified: arXiv 1003.0064, Stehlé's page]. Klein's randomized nearest plane is analyzed and its decoding radius optimized, beating the original Klein. An efficient randomized-rounding implementation is included, and a moderate number of samples gets near-ML up to dimension 32. — [arXiv 1003.0064](https://web3.arxiv.org/abs/1003.0064); [Stehlé page](https://perso.ens-lyon.fr/damien.stehle/RandLatDec.html)
- **Wang, Ling, "Lattice Gaussian Sampling by Markov Chain Monte Carlo: Bounded Distance Decoding and Trapdoor Sampling," IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 65(6), June 2019; arXiv 1704.02673; IACR ePrint 2019/660** [index-verified]. Independent Metropolis-Hastings-Klein with convergence analysis, a decoding-radius-versus-complexity trade-off for BDD, application to trapdoor sampling, and a parallelizable independent multiple-try Metropolis-Klein variant. — [arXiv 1704.02673](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.02673); [ePrint 2019/660](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/660)
- Lindner-Peikert footnote 4 [opened] contrasts NearestPlanes with Klein's algorithm. Klein's guarantee depends only on the shortest GS vector, whereas NearestPlanes is tailored to a known error distribution. — [PDF](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~cpeikert/pubs/lwe-analysis.pdf)
- Aono-Nguyen 2017 (discrete pruning) formalizes random sampling as a form of enumeration in the SVP setting. — [ePrint 2017/155](https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/155)
- Crypto cross-check: a search for "Gibbs sampling / MCMC lattice decoding attack LWE" found no MCMC/Gibbs LWE attack. The nearest statistical work is the "Cool & Cruel" sparse-secret attack in the benchmarking paper, which is not MCMC. — [Benchmarking Attacks on LWE, arXiv 2408.00882](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.00882)
- Adjacent 2026 work: **Jiang, "When the Learning With Errors Problem Meets the Coherent Ising Machine: A Penalty-Free Algorithm-Hardware Co-Design," arXiv 2606.22843 (June 2026)** [opened]. Maps LWE-BDD to a penalty-free QUBO using relaxed Babai projection and mixed-radix encoding, and runs end to end on a CIM for a 40-dimensional TU Darmstadt LWE challenge instance. It does not cite or connect to MIMO Ising-based detectors. — [arXiv 2606.22843](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22843)

### Inferences
- **For the strongly decaying BKZ profile, Klein sampling is a poor fit as the main decoder.** Lindner-Peikert's own footnote makes the point: per-level deterministic allocation (or K-best) puts trials exactly on the weak levels. Klein randomizes all levels, including the near-noiseless top levels where the right choice is obvious, which wastes nothing but also gains nothing there.
- **Where MCMC could add something new.** Use Metropolis-Klein (Wang-Ling) *as a refinement / restart mechanism* around the K-best survivor list. The chain's stationary distribution is the lattice Gaussian centred on the target, so it explores neighbourhoods of the best K-best leaves without a full tree restart. The multiple-try variant parallelizes. Not found in crypto attack literature, so this is a candidate novelty, but expected gains at dim 100-1000 with sigma ≈ 1 are uncertain (Wang-Ling's analysis shows mixing time grows with how far the target is beyond the decoding radius).
- **Ising/QUBO.** The 2026 CIM paper shows the QUBO view of LWE-BDD is being explored. MIMO has a separate literature on Ising/annealing detectors (not verified here), which could be a further "transfer" source. It is not relevant at cryptographic dimensions now.

### Gaps
- No crypto paper was found comparing Klein/Liu-Ling-Stehlé sampling with Lindner-Peikert or pruned enumeration on BKZ-reduced LWE bases at dimension ≥ 100.
- Wang-Ling's full text was not opened. Their BDD trade-off formula was not checked against LWE parameters.
- [UNVERIFIED] Klein, "Finding the closest lattice vector when it's unusually close," SODA 2000 (not opened).

## Q4. Learned decoders (DetNet, OAMP-Net, DL-aided sphere decoding / K-best) and neural CVP/LWE

### Takeaway
Learned MIMO detectors are mature, but no paper was found that applies learned pruning, learned ordering, or deep-unfolded detectors to BDD in an LWE primal attack. ML work on LWE (the SALSA family) trains transformers end to end on (a, b) pairs, which is a different paradigm. Theory on neural CVP decoders suggests shallow networks need exponential size for general lattices.

### Cited Findings
- **Samuel, Diskin, Wiesel, "Learning to Detect," IEEE TSP 67(10):2554-2564, 2019** (DOI 10.1109/TSP.2019.2899805) [index-verified: arXiv 1805.07631, dblp]. DetNet unfolds projected gradient descent into a network and extends to soft output. — [arXiv 1805.07631](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.07631v1.pdf); [Wiesel homepage](https://www.cs.huji.ac.il/~amiw)
- **He, Wen, Jin, Li, "Model-Driven Deep Learning for MIMO Detection," IEEE TSP 68:1702-1715, 2020** [index-verified: arXiv 1907.09439]. OAMP-Net2 unfolds OAMP with a few trainable parameters whose count is independent of the number of antennas, and it outperforms other DL detectors. — [arXiv 1907.09439](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.09439); [IEEE SPS newsletter](https://signalprocessingsociety.org/newsletter/2022/07/model-driven-deep-learning-mimo-detection)
- **Nguyen, Lee, Dai, "Application of Deep Learning to Sphere Decoding for Large MIMO Systems," IEEE TWC (DOI 10.1109/TWC.2021.3076527); arXiv 2010.13481** [opened]. A DNN supplies a highly reliable initial candidate, combined with candidate and layer ordering plus early rejection. FDL-SD gives >90% complexity cut at 24x24 QPSK with no loss. FDL-KSD with K=32 matches conventional K-best with K=256 at 32x32. — [arXiv 2010.13481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.13481)
- **Corlay et al., "Neural Lattice Decoders," arXiv 1807.00592** [index-verified title only; author list UNVERIFIED]. Builds an optimal decoder from Boolean equations and Voronoi facets and introduces "Voronoi-reduced" bases. A related arXiv 1902.05146 argues that a two-layer ReLU network needs exponentially many neurons to solve CVP while deep networks can be polynomial for structured (root) lattices. — [arXiv 1807.00592](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.00592); [arXiv 1902.05146](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1902.05146)
- **Wenger, Chen, Charton, Lauter, "SALSA: Attacking Lattice Cryptography with Transformers," NeurIPS 2022; ePrint 2022/935** [index-verified]. Transformers trained on modular arithmetic plus statistical secret recovery, effective on small-to-mid LWE with sparse binary secrets. — [ePrint 2022/935](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/935); [NeurIPS](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/e28b3369186459f57c94a9ec9137fac9-Abstract.html)
- **"Benchmarking Attacks on Learning with Errors," arXiv 2408.00882** [index-verified; author list UNVERIFIED, a Meta AI plus academic team]. Compares uSVP, SALSA, Cool & Cruel and Dual-Hybrid MitM. It reports the first secret recovery on standardized Kyber-type MLWE (ranks 2 and 3, binomial distributions) but only for *small/sparse secret* variants. — [arXiv 2408.00882](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.00882)
- Crypto cross-check: the search "deep learning lattice CVP LWE neural decoding attack" found no neural BDD/CVP decoder used in an LWE primal attack.

### Inferences
- **Most transferable piece: learned pruning or learned initial candidate in the K-best stage, not a learned end-to-end decoder.** Nguyen-Lee-Dai's gain (K=32 matching K=256) comes from a better first candidate plus ordering. For LWE, the "features" per level are scale-free (y_i/||b*_i||, residual energy so far, level index relative to the GSA slope). A small model could be trained on synthetic BKZ-reduced LWE instances and predict per-level keep/drop probabilities, replacing a hand-tuned T-threshold. This looks novel in crypto. The risk is that the gain over an analytically optimal pruning schedule (GNR/Lindner-Peikert-style, computable from the GSA) is small, because the BDD tree statistics here are almost exactly Gaussian per level and the optimal rule is analytic.
- DetNet/OAMP-Net rely on i.i.d.-like channel matrices and finite constellations. BKZ-reduced bases are highly structured and the integer coordinates are unbounded, so direct transfer is unlikely to work. The Corlay et al. expressivity results support skepticism.

### Gaps
- No paper was found on learned pruning thresholds for lattice enumeration in cryptanalysis. "Learned pruning functions" for BKZ/enumeration may exist without matching the queries used. Not established.

## Q5. Exploiting the known discrete, bounded error prior (MAP vs ML, box-constrained SD, infinity-norm pruning)

### Takeaway
The crypto primal attack nearly always treats CBD error as Gaussian and uses the Euclidean metric, which is ML for Gaussian noise. MIMO has well-developed box-constrained ILS (BILS) and infinity-norm sphere decoding. The crypto side has hint-based Bayesian frameworks and key-enumeration with non-uniform priors, but none was found that puts a discrete CBD prior or a box/infinity-norm test *inside the BDD tree search* after BKZ. This is a plausible novelty, but there is a structural obstacle: the box sits on the error/secret coordinates, and unimodular reduction destroys box structure in the integer variables.

### Inferences
- **MAP metric in the tree (most concrete novelty candidate in this question).** With a basis that keeps the error coordinates explicit (Kannan/Bai-Galbraith embedding), each leaf gives a full candidate error e. A K-best decoder can then rank leaves (and, with care, partial nodes) by the CBD log-prior Σ log P_CBD(e_j) instead of ||e||². Partial nodes need care because partial GS coordinates are linear combinations of e_j, so the per-coordinate prior is not separable along the tree. The required changes are:
  - **Exact leaf rescoring**, which is cheap and clearly valid: re-rank the final K-best list by CBD likelihood.
  - **Hard infeasibility test**: any candidate with |e_j| > eta for some j has probability zero under CBD(eta). This is an infinity-norm box test on the *original* coordinates, applicable at leaves and as a bound on partially determined coordinates when the embedding keeps some coordinates triangular.

  For CBD(2) the support is {-2,...,2}. For a fixed squared norm, the Gaussian metric cannot tell a vector with one ±3 apart from several ±1s, but CBD rules the first out. This matters most exactly at the hard settings where several leaves have similar ||e||².
- **Box constraint vs reduction.** In MIMO, LR-aided detection loses the constellation boundary, and BILS (Chang-Han) keeps the box by using reduction that respects it. In LWE the box sits on s and e. After BKZ, the tree's integer variables are coefficients in the reduced basis and carry no box. The box survives only as a constraint on the residual t - Bx. So BILS does not transfer directly, while infinity-norm residual checks (Seethaler-Bölcskei flavour, applied to un-rotated coordinates) do.
- **Soft hints loop.** Per-coordinate CBD posteriors from a list decoder could be turned into DDGR "approximate hints" and fed back to rebuild a lattice. No crypto paper doing this iteration was found.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that uses a CBD (or any discrete) prior or a box/infinity-norm test inside a primal-attack BDD tree. The full texts of 2023/139 and 2023/1547 were not checked for such a step.
- Whether infinity-norm pruning in rotated (QR) coordinates gives any guarantee for a box in original coordinates was not established from sources.

## Q6. Cross-check summary: has each technique been used in lattice cryptanalysis?

### Takeaway
Already present in crypto in substance:
- LR-aided detection (BKZ itself)
- MMSE augmentation (Kannan/Bai-Galbraith embedding with scaling)
- fixed per-level branching / FSD (Lindner-Peikert NearestPlanes)
- probabilistic/statistical pruning (GNR extreme pruning, Liu-Nguyen BDD enumeration, Aono-Nguyen discrete pruning)
- Klein sampling (known, GPV; decoding-by-sampling co-authored by Stehlé)

Not found in any LWE cryptanalysis paper:
- breadth-first K-best/M-algorithm with a global survivor list
- best-first stack/Fano/A* with a bias-corrected metric
- list/soft-output sphere decoding feeding a second stage
- MCMC (Metropolis-Klein) refinement as an attack component
- learned pruning or learned initial candidates for the BDD tree
- discrete-prior (CBD) MAP scoring or box/infinity-norm feasibility inside the tree

### Inferences
Ranked by (novelty × plausible benefit on BKZ-reduced, low-noise, CBD-error LWE BDD):
1. **CBD-prior MAP rescoring plus infinity-norm/box feasibility** at the leaves of the K-best list, possibly also on partially determined original coordinates. Cheap, clearly not found in crypto, and targets exactly the hard-setting ties.
2. **Best-first (stack/Fano) search with a Fano bias term computed from the per-level known sigma** (Murugan et al.). Not found in crypto, and it could cut node count relative to K-best at equal success, because K-best wastes width on easy levels.
3. **Soft-output list decoding feeding a hybrid/hints stage** (turbo-style iteration with DDGR approximate hints or with guessing of the least reliable coordinates). Most novel, but the most engineering.
4. **Adaptive K / ESD-style radius schedule** tied to the GSA profile, giving a predictable cost model.
5. **Learned per-level pruning / DNN initial candidate.** Novel in crypto, but likely small gain over analytically optimal pruning.
6. **Metropolis-Klein MCMC refinement around survivors.** Novel as an attack component, uncertain benefit.

Not worth pursuing as "novel": SQRD/V-BLAST reordering after BKZ (can hurt, per Chang-Wen-Xie), MMSE augmentation (already the scaled embedding), FSD (≈ Lindner-Peikert), plain Shim-Kang/Gowaikar-Hassibi probabilistic pruning (≈ GNR/Liu-Nguyen pruning).
